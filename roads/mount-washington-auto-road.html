Mount Washington Auto Road, NH

Never Drive Mount Washington Before You Know This Rule

Road File · Published September 2026 · Facts checked against the sources below

The short version

The Mount Washington Auto Road climbs eight miles to the 6,288-foot summit of the highest peak in the northeastern United States. Average grade twelve percent, sustained. Two-way traffic on a narrow road, long sections of gravel, and for most of its length no guardrail.

It opened in 1861, for horse-drawn carriages, and the alignment is essentially unchanged. There is also a rule most drivers have never heard of: the weight carried inside the vehicle is limited, and the figure used is 900 pounds. Weight above that strains the transmission in low gear, drivers back out of the gear and use the brake pedal instead, and that is the sequence that destroys brakes on this mountain.

Timeline

  • 1861 — The road opens for horse-drawn carriages.
  • April 12, 1934 — Observers record a 231 mph gust at the summit.
  • Today — Around 250,000 people come up the road each year.

Before you drive it

  • Use the lowest gear and stay under 20 mph, as the toll-house instructions say.
  • Keep the load inside the vehicle under 900 pounds: driver, passengers and luggage.
  • The descent is the dangerous direction. If you smell your brakes, pull over and stop.
  • Check the road status and the summit forecast the morning you travel.
