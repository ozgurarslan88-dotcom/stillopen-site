I-75 · Mackinac Bridge, MI

Mackinac Bridge Has Staff Who Will Drive Your Car Across For You

Road File · Published October 2026 · Facts checked against the sources below

The short version

The Mackinac Bridge carries Interstate 75 five miles across the Straits of Mackinac in Michigan, between Mackinaw City and St. Ignace. It opened in November 1957. The two centre lanes are open steel grating, so you can see Lake Michigan through the road.

That grating is the reason it is safe. After the Tacoma Narrows Bridge tore itself apart in a 40 mph wind in 1940, engineers built this deck to let the wind through. It is also why people are afraid of it. About a thousand drivers a year hand over their keys at the toll plaza, and a bridge employee drives them across in their own car.

Timeline

  • 1940 — Tacoma Narrows Bridge collapses in a 40 mph wind; the lesson shapes the Mackinac design.
  • November 1957 — Mackinac Bridge opens.
  • 1959 — First Labor Day Bridge Walk, the one day a year people may cross on foot.
  • Today — Wind rules in four stages: over 20 mph high-profile vehicles held to 20; over 35 escorted; over 50 banned; at 65 the bridge closes.

Before you drive it

  • Check the bridge authority's conditions before you go, especially if you are towing or driving anything tall. Restrictions change through the day as the wind does.
  • In a pickup, during partial closures the bed has to be empty and the cover has to come off.
  • Turn your headlights on, slow down, and use the outside lanes in high wind. They are solid, not grating.
  • Do not stop on the bridge. Every day but the Labor Day walk, the bridge is a road.
  • If you reach the toll plaza and cannot do it, say so. Drivers and passengers must be 18 or over, there is a waiver to sign, and somebody will take the wheel.
