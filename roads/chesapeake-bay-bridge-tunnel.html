U.S. 13 · Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel, VA

Why Some Drivers Get Turned Away at the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel

Road File · Published October 2026 · Facts checked against the sources below

The short version

The Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel in Virginia runs seventeen miles across the open mouth of the bay, and dives under the sea twice. On a windy day, a sign at the toll plaza decides whether your vehicle is allowed across at all: campers, rental moving trucks and motorcycles are the first to be turned away.

Around eighteen vehicles have gone over the side since it opened in 1964. The video looks at the ship strikes, the wind rules, the trucks that went into the bay, and the stretch under the sea where nothing separates you from oncoming traffic.

Timeline

  • 1964 — The bridge-tunnel opens.
  • Wind days — Level 1 to Level 4 restrictions turn away campers, rental trucks and motorcycles first.
  • Ongoing — A parallel tunnel program is replacing two-way traffic in the tunnels, through about 2040.

Before you drive it

  • Check the crossing's wind status before you leave, not at the toll plaza. A Level 1 restriction turns around rental moving trucks, campers and motorcycles.
  • Coming back the same way? With E-ZPass, a return crossing within 24 hours costs far less.
  • On the bridges, stay in your lane, slow down in wind, do not pass where it is banned, and do not sit alongside a truck.
  • In the tunnels the traffic beside you is coming toward you. Keep right, headlights on, steady speed, never pass.
  • If you break down, hazards on, stay in the vehicle with your seatbelt on unless it is unsafe, and call the crossing's own police.
