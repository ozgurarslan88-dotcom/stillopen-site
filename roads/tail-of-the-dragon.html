U.S. 129 · Tail of the Dragon, TN/NC

Never Drive the Tail of the Dragon on a Weekend

Road File · Published August 2026 · Facts checked against the sources below

The short version

U.S. Highway 129 packs more than three hundred curves into eleven miles along the Tennessee and North Carolina line. There are no intersecting roads, no driveways, no houses and no businesses on the entire stretch. Once you are on it, you stay on it until it ends.

The road bends that way because 1930s engineers followed old trail lines along the ridges instead of tunnelling or grading the mountain. For fifty years almost nobody drove it. Today it draws a quarter of a million riders a year, and in 2024 alone it produced 112 crashes, 47 injuries and five deaths.

Timeline

  • 1930s — The road is built along old ridge trail lines to avoid tunnelling and grading.
  • December 2014 — Tennessee bans vehicles over 30 feet long from the Dragon.
  • 2024 — 112 crashes, 47 injuries and five deaths.
  • May 2026 — The first fatal crash of 2026 on the Dragon.

Before you drive it

  • If you can, drive it on a weekday morning. Weekends bring the heaviest motorcycle and sports-car traffic.
  • Stay fully in your lane in every curve. Most of the bends are blind, and there is no shoulder.
  • Expect a vehicle or rider stopped around any curve, and keep your speed to what you can stop from in the distance you can see.
  • Vehicles over 30 feet are not allowed. Plan another route for a long RV or trailer.
  • There are no services or side roads on the 11 miles. Start with fuel and water.
