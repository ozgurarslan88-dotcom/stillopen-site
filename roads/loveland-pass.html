U.S. 6 · Loveland Pass, CO

Never Drive Loveland Pass Behind a Fuel Truck

Road File · Published September 2026 · Facts checked against the sources below

The short version

Sixty miles west of Denver, Interstate 70 goes into a mountain. The Eisenhower-Johnson Memorial Tunnel carries it under the Continental Divide, with the west portal at 11,158 feet, the highest point on the Interstate system. Trucks carrying placarded hazardous materials cannot use it.

Instead, those loads go over the top. Loveland Pass on U.S. 6 climbs to roughly 11,990 feet on steep grades and tight hairpins, and reporting puts the number of hazmat trucks using it at around two hundred a day. When storms or avalanche control close the pass, Colorado stops all other traffic, empties the tunnel, and escorts the waiting hazmat trucks through on their own.

Timeline

  • March 1973 — The westbound tunnel bore opens.
  • 1979 — The eastbound bore opens.
  • Winter closures — When the pass closes, hazmat trucks are escorted through an emptied tunnel at the top of the hour.
  • June 2025 — A mountain slide forces an extended closure of Loveland Pass.

Before you drive it

  • Check CDOT road conditions before you go. Loveland Pass closes frequently in winter.
  • Expect fuel tankers and other hazmat trucks on the grades. Give them room and never cut in front on a descent.
  • Use a low gear on the way down and watch for the hairpins.
  • If the pass is closed, expect delays in the tunnel while hazmat trucks are escorted through.
