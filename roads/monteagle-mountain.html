I-24 · Monteagle Mountain, TN

Why Truckers Fear the East Side of Monteagle Mountain

Road File · Published October 2026 · Facts checked against the sources below

The short version

On Interstate 24 over Monteagle Mountain in Tennessee, the eastbound descent drops more than a thousand feet in about four miles, and both runaway truck ramps are on the left. A truck that has lost its brakes has to cross two lanes of traffic to reach one.

In the early 1980s this one descent produced more than fifty crashes a year. A few years later the count was down to three. The video looks at what happens inside a truck's brakes on a grade like this, why the eastbound lanes are really the old U.S. 41, and the change at the top of the mountain that made the difference.

Timeline

  • Old U.S. 41 — The eastbound lanes follow the alignment of the old mountain highway.
  • Early 1980s — More than fifty crashes a year on the eastbound descent.
  • A few years later — After changes at the top of the mountain, the count falls to three.
  • Today — Runaway ramps remain on the left side; trucks must stop at the inspection station.

Before you drive it

  • In a car heading east, slow down at the top and keep to the posted 55 all the way down. The speed difference with slow trucks is the danger.
  • Do not cut in close in front of a truck on the descent. It may already be using everything it has.
  • Watch the left side near the runaway ramps. A truck flashing its lights and moving left is out of options; get out of its way.
  • Check Tennessee SmartWay for closures before you set off. A crash on the grade can shut one or both directions for hours.
  • In a heavy vehicle, motorhome or with a heavy trailer, choose your gear before the grade and let the engine do the work.
  • If you drive a truck, stop at the inspection station like the signs say.
