I-26 · Saluda Grade, NC

Why Truckers Fear the Saluda Grade on I-26 in North Carolina

Road File · Published November 2026 · Facts checked against the sources below

The short version

In Polk County, North Carolina, a few miles above the South Carolina line, the Blue Ridge Mountains end and Interstate 26 has to get down the drop. Heading east, it falls into the Green River gorge at a 7% grade, crosses the river on twin bridges about 225 feet high, then leaves Howard Gap on a 6% downhill that runs for about three miles toward Columbus.

Right beside it is the old Saluda Grade railroad, for more than a century the steepest main line railroad grade in the United States. Norfolk Southern closed it in 2001. The interstate stayed open, and between 2010 and 2020 the state recorded 340 crashes on one three-mile stretch of it. The fatality rate there was lower than the North Carolina interstate average; the crash rate was slightly higher.

Timeline

  • July 4, 1878 — The railroad over the Saluda Grade opens.
  • Oct 29, 1976 — I-26 through Polk County is dedicated and opens to traffic.
  • Dec 1998 — A truck carrying an industrial chemical overturns and burns on the grade; the interstate is partly closed for days.
  • Dec 2001 — Norfolk Southern stops running trains over the railroad grade.
  • Oct 2011 — A driver jumps from his moving rig on the grade; it rolls on and causes a multi-truck crash.
  • 2022 — The Tryon fire chief reports two to three crashes a week at mile markers 62 and 63 eastbound.
  • Aug 2025 — NCDOT extends its free IMAP roadside assistance patrol on I-26 to the South Carolina line.
  • Jan 2026 — Tractor-trailers jackknife on the grade in snow during a winter storm.
  • Oct 26, 2026 — A truck carrying hazardous materials overturns; part of I-26 East is shut down.

Before you drive it

  • Check DriveNC or call 511 for I-26 in Polk County before you go, especially in winter or when fog is forecast.
  • It can be snowing and icy at Saluda when it is only cold rain down in Columbus.
  • If you drive a truck, gear down before you start down from Howard Gap, not halfway through, and do not ride your brakes.
  • In a car, give the trucks room on the grade and never cut in front of a loaded rig on the way down.
  • Stay off the shoulder edge. The shoulders slope away, and a wheel that drops off the pavement can pull you off the road.
  • If you break down on the grade, call for help. NCDOT's IMAP roadside assistance covers this stretch and is free.
