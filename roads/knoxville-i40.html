I-40 · Knoxville, TN

Why Tennessee Shut Down I-40 Through Knoxville for 14 Months

Road File · Published November 2026 · Facts checked against the sources below

The short version

At 12:01 a.m. on May 1, 2008, Tennessee closed Interstate 40 through downtown Knoxville on purpose and sent through traffic around the city on I-640. The section carried about 103,000 vehicles a day, and at one point five lanes of traffic merged into two. The state's transportation commissioner said it held one of the worst safety records in the nation.

Rebuilding it with traffic still running was expected to take more than eight years. Closing it let crews work around the clock. The road reopened on June 12, 2009, eighteen days ahead of schedule. Today the bottleneck has moved west, to the 17 miles where I-40 and I-75 share the same pavement.

Timeline

  • 1945 — Knoxville's freeway plan first proposes a northern loop, the future I-640.
  • Mid-1970s — The I-40/I-75 cloverleaf earns the nickname "Malfunction Junction."
  • 1980–1982 — About $250 million rebuild ahead of the 1982 World's Fair; I-640 completed in December 1981.
  • 1987 — Trucks with hazardous materials barred from I-40 through the city unless delivering there; I-640 designated instead.
  • April 2004 — A workshop of 82 highway professionals from 19 states recommends a full closure.
  • May 1, 2008 — I-40 closed between James White Parkway and Hall of Fame Drive.
  • June 12, 2009 — Reopened 18 days early: six through lanes, four auxiliary lanes, about $190 million.
  • February 19, 2026 — Five-vehicle chain-reaction crash in stopped traffic on I-40 West near Cedar Bluff Road; one driver killed.
  • November 2, 2026 — TDOT releases its West Knoxville corridor study: widening or Choice Lanes. Not final; not funded.

Before you drive it

  • Check Tennessee SmartWay or call 511 for I-40 and I-75 through Knoxville.
  • Avoid West Knoxville on weekday afternoons, roughly 2:30–6:30 p.m., when traffic often slows to 31 mph or less.
  • Leave plenty of room ahead. Rear-end crashes in stopped traffic are a major danger here.
  • Expect slow merges: many acceleration lanes on the west side are short.
  • Just passing through? I-640 around the north side is often simpler.
  • Hauling hazardous materials? You may not use I-40 through Knoxville unless you are delivering or loading there. Use I-640.
