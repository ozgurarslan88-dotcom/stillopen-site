I-8 · Mountain Springs Grade, CA

This California Interstate Tells You to Turn Off Your AC

Road File · Published November 2026 · Facts checked against the sources below

The short version

On the line between San Diego County and Imperial County, Interstate 8 climbs out of the desert near Ocotillo and rises nearly three thousand feet in about eleven miles. At the bottom of the westbound climb, a yellow sign reads: Avoid overheating. Turn off A/C. Next 10 miles. Summer temperatures at the foot of the grade often top 110°F.

The two directions of the freeway split into different canyons, more than a mile apart in places: westbound climbs through Devils Canyon, eastbound comes down through In-Ko-Pah Gorge on the bed of the old highway. The grade reaches about 7.5% above Mountain Springs, there is no truck climbing lane, and strong winds close the freeway to high-profile vehicles several times a year.

Timeline

  • 1867 — A wagon road climbs more than 800 feet in less than a mile, averaging about 18%; going down could take a whole day.
  • 1870 — A toll road rebuilds the grade; Mountain Springs can now be reached in twenty minutes.
  • April 1913 — San Diego County opens an automobile road down Myer Canyon, 6.5 miles long with a 7% maximum grade.
  • 1963–1965 — Interstate 8 is built on the grade, with westbound lanes in a new alignment through Devils Canyon.
  • Sept 1976 — Tropical Storm Kathleen washes out the eastbound road; I-8 is closed for seven days.
  • March 2012 — A rare ice storm closes I-8 between Alpine and Ocotillo for about 7.5 hours.
  • Aug 2023 — Remnants of Hurricane Hilary bring a rock slide that closes the freeway at In-Ko-Pah.
  • Jan 2025 — Gusts of about 70 mph close I-8 between Alpine and Ocotillo to high-profile vehicles for about five hours.
  • Aug 2025 — Caltrans files plans to replace a damaged wind fence near In-Ko-Pah Park Road.

Before you drive it

  • Check Caltrans QuickMap or 511 San Diego before you go, especially in summer heat or when strong winds are forecast.
  • In summer, cross the grade in the morning, and check your coolant before you leave.
  • Watch your temperature gauge on the climb. If it rises, turn off the air conditioning; if it keeps rising, pull off safely and let the engine cool. Never open a hot radiator cap.
  • Carry your own drinking water, plus extra water or coolant for the radiator.
  • Driving a motor home, box truck or towing a trailer? Check wind warnings first. High-profile vehicles are the first to be turned back.
  • Coming down eastbound, use a lower gear, watch for slow trucks in the right lane, and know where the runaway truck ramp is.
