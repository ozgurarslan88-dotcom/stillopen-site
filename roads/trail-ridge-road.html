U.S. 34 · Trail Ridge Road, CO

Never Drive Trail Ridge Road in Rocky Mountain National Park After Noon

Road File · Published September 2026 · Facts checked against the sources below

The short version

Trail Ridge Road crosses Rocky Mountain National Park in Colorado and reaches 12,183 feet, the highest continuous paved road in the United States. It stays above the treeline for eleven miles, with no trees, almost no buildings, and long stretches with no guardrail.

That is what makes the afternoons dangerous. Storms build over the Continental Divide through the late morning and break in the early afternoon, and from the high point it takes twenty minutes or more to drive back down to shelter. On two consecutive days in July 2014, lightning killed two visitors at stops along the road less than two miles apart.

Timeline

  • 1930s — Trail Ridge Road is built across the high ground of the park.
  • July 11, 2014 — Lightning kills a visitor near the Ute Crossing trailhead.
  • July 12, 2014 — Lightning kills a second visitor at Rainbow Curve, less than two miles away.
  • 2015 — The National Weather Service publishes a meteorological analysis of both deaths.

Before you drive it

  • Plan to be over the high section and back below the treeline by noon in summer.
  • Watch the sky over the Continental Divide from late morning. If clouds are building, turn around early.
  • If a storm arrives, get back in your car with the doors closed. Do not stand at overlooks or on open tundra.
  • Check the park's road status before you go; the road opens late and closes early in the season.
