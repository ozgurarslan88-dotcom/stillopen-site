I-80 · Laramie to Rawlins, WY

Why Wyoming Begged Washington Not to Build This Part of I-80

Road File · Published October 2026 · Facts checked against the sources below

The short version

In 1956, federal planners found a way to cut nineteen miles off the route across southern Wyoming. Wyoming said no. Locals warned the ground near Elk Mountain buried everything in drifting snow, and a century earlier the Union Pacific had gone around it. The interstate was built there anyway. Four days after it opened in 1970, a storm closed it.

Truckers named it the Snow Chi Minh Trail. Today Interstate 80 between Laramie and Rawlins is one of the busiest freight corridors in the country, and it still closes behind red and white gates.

Timeline

  • 1860s — The Union Pacific railroad routes around the Elk Mountain area.
  • 1956 — Federal planners choose the shorter route over Wyoming's objections.
  • 1970 — The section opens; four days later a storm closes it.
  • Today — One of the busiest freight corridors in the U.S., with frequent winter closures.

Before you drive it

  • Check the road report before you set off and again during the trip. Wyoming publishes closures, weight restrictions and live wind speeds.
  • Towing, driving a camper, or hauling an empty trailer? Take the weight restrictions literally. They are written for you.
  • In blowing snow or fog, slow down long before you lose the view.
  • Carry winter gear even on a clear morning: blankets, water, food, a full tank.
  • If the closure arms are down, do not go around them.
